# Read a Find Capacity Result

Find Capacity climbs a staircase of checkpoint rates and reports where the Target stopped holding: Find Capacity steps from ×0.25 to ×3 of the nominal rate through its checkpoint rates, moving to each in 5 seconds and holding it, and ends the search when aggregate errors exceed 5%, or 5 × the SLO error ratio when that is larger. Checkpoints are spaced geometrically on the aggregate RPS of the searched range (O-60): from its start they multiply by 2 while the next one stays at or below the end ÷ √2, then the end itself, so 5 → 150 RPS gives 10, 20, 40, 80 and 150. A range too narrow for 4 such checkpoints gets 4 checkpoints with the equal ratio (end ÷ start)^(1/4); a ramp from 0 gets end ÷ 2ᵏ for the same count. The latency stop is 1.5 × the latency reference. When the first confirmed failure comes before any checkpoint passed, the next range is from ÷ 4 to the scheduled rate of that failure (the stop's scheduled rate at its trigger, or the failing checkpoint), and the Result states what was observed there: the scheduled and delivered rate, p95 and error ratio of the stop when it failed, otherwise of the failing checkpoint. Each checkpoint is judged over its own hold of the staircase after the settle, never over the transition to it (O-85). The checkpoint profile lists checkpoints by ascending rate. A stop is the failure of the staircase step it fired in, at that step's checkpoint rate and marked as a stop, never below a checkpoint that held. Above the searched range the next range is to to 2 × to. The knee is the first checkpoint at 2 × the median p95 of the first 3 passing checkpoints; a Load Test at 80% of the limit confirms it.

Each step of the staircase is a short transition from the previous rate (`rise_k`, a ramp), then a hold at the checkpoint rate (`hold_k`) whose first `settleSeconds` are not measured; the rest of the hold is that checkpoint's window. Moving to a rate is never judged, only holding it. Resolve sizes every hold so it meets the sample minimum at its rate and gives the remaining traffic time mostly to the upper holds, near the limit: hold k of n gets k of n(n + 1)/2 shares. A `trafficSeconds` too short for every step is refused with `objective_invalid` at `/objective/knobs/trafficSeconds`, and the message names the minimum; no step is dropped. With the default 300 s, every step is a 5 s transition and a 15 s settle; 5 → 150 RPS then measures 31, 32, 34, 36 and 37 s, and 2 → 30 RPS measures 37, 44, 51 and 58 s.

The resolved Load Spec already lists the checkpoint rates and their holds, before any Load Plan is compiled or confirmed: `measurements.checkpoint.over.checkpoints[]` with `rps` and the hold's `stageId` in `capacitylab.load_specs.resolve` and `capacitylab.load_specs.get` (5 → 150 RPS gives 10, 20, 40, 80, 150; 2 → 30 gives 4, 8, 16, 30), and `traffic.stages` shows each step's durations. The default `to` is 3 × the nominal rate; to find where the Target breaks, propose `to: { rps: <the Target's maxPeakRps> }`, the ceiling the member set. Tell the member these rates and how long each is held when asking them to approve the run; the Result's `checkpoint_profile` is judged at exactly these rates, never at a grid recomputed later.

The search stops are armed over the whole staircase, transitions included, and "sustained for `stopSustainSeconds`" means held throughout: every group of 5-second monitor ticks with enough samples in the window must breach, so a healthy hold inside the window prevents the stop. A stop is the failure of the step it fired in, its transition, settle or hold: `capacity_limit.firstFailRps` is then that step's checkpoint rate, marked `firstFailAt: "stop"`, never a failing rate below the last one held. `firstFailHold` says how far that step's hold got: `not_reached` ("broke while stepping up to 20 RPS, before that rate was measured"), `not_measured` ("broke at 20 RPS while it settled, before it was measured") or `measured_too_briefly` ("broke at 20 RPS before it was measured long enough to judge"). A `not_run` entry of the `checkpoint_profile` carries the same `notRunReason`; tell the member a hold that was reached apart from one never reached. `stop_rate` then compares the rate scheduled over the stop condition's sustain window with the rate delivered over the same window, the window of the stop's `checkpoint@stop` row; requests abandoned in flight at the stop are `abandoned_at_stop`, not part of it.

Read the Result with `capacitylab.results.get` and its outcome from the findings:

| Outcome                       | Verdict                              | What it says                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `bracketed`                   | `attained`                           | At least one checkpoint passed, then one failed and was confirmed: `capacity_limit{lastPassRps, firstFailRps}`, the limiting operation and the knee.                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `below_start`                 | `not_attained`                       | A confirmed failure came before any checkpoint passed: `failure_observed{at, scheduledRps, deliveredRps, p95Ms, errorRatio}` says what the Target did there, with a `next_range` from below `from` up to the scheduled rate of that failure, or `functional_failure` when authentication, redirect or other 4xx errors dominate — run a Smoke and fix the Workload instead. |
| `at_least`                    | `not_attained`                       | Every checkpoint through the top of the range passed: a higher `next_range`, `requires_paid_level` at the level cap, or `beyond_product_ceiling` at the product ceiling.                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `unconfirmed_degradation`     | `not_attained`                       | Only the last checkpoint failed and nothing confirmed it: `top_edge_signal` and a `next_range` around that point.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `missing_checkpoint_evidence` | `inconclusive`                       | The walk stopped at missing evidence; `at_least(lastPassRps)` when a checkpoint passed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `stopped_before_bracket`      | `inconclusive` (cause `user`)        | A member stop ended the search first; `at_least(lastPassRps)`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| system stop before a bracket  | `inconclusive` (cause `capacitylab`) | An operator stop, deadline or execution fault ended the search; a bracket found before an operator stop stays `attained`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

When telling the member about a `below_start` Result, state what was observed, not only the next range: for example "at a scheduled 31.92 RPS the Target was sent 17 RPS at p95 ≤ 3000 ms; search next between 1.25 and 25 RPS". `failure_observed.at` is `stop` when the stop that ended the search failed (its delivered and scheduled rates are over the stop condition's sustain window, like `stop_rate`), otherwise `checkpoint`. The `checkpoint_profile` lists checkpoints by ascending rate; the stop appears at the rate of the step it failed, right after that step's hold.

The knee never changes the verdict; the Result shows the limit and the knee side by side. A limit is confirmed best with a Load Test at a fraction of it, as the finding recommends.

A `next_range` is advice for a **new** Load Spec. Nothing is resolved, compiled or confirmed automatically. When a stop condition ended the search early, the unused traffic of the plan is refunded with the Result (`billing.status` `partially_refunded`, category `unused_traffic`); the launch fee and the traffic up to the stop are kept. If the member wants to continue, resolve a new Load Spec with that range, compile a Load Plan and obtain the member's explicit approval of that exact plan digest and US-dollar price before `capacitylab.runs.confirm`.
